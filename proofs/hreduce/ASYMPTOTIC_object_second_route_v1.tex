\subsection*{An asymptotic object: what the rows and the band imply}
Write \(\mu=ma>0\) for the forming species, \(g=G/a^2\),
\(r_s=2GE\), \(\chi=a/r_s\), and
\(w=v/N<0\). The radial rows are declared; the following deductions
have their stated smooth-source, positive-clock domains. Size scalings
keep \(g,\mu\) fixed. They do not complete the core's
hydrodynamically invalid evolution.

\paragraph{1. Compressed energy and clock.}
In a positive local band-diagonal description let \(n\ge0\),
\(\mathscr N'=4\pi r^2n\), and assume
\(\rho=n\epsilon_c/a\) with constant mean \(\epsilon_c\), and
\(\mathcal M=m\mathscr N\), with no
omitted signed-reference or averaging terms in these identities.
The two radial rows then give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 J=\frac{n(\epsilon_c-\mu)}{a|w|},\qquad
 \frac{N'}N=\frac{\epsilon_c-\mu}{2\mu}
              \frac{\mathscr N'}{\mathscr N},\\
 N=N_R\left(\frac{\mathscr N}{\mathscr N_R}\right)^{(\epsilon_c-\mu)/(2\mu)}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The momentum source \(J\) is outward when \(\epsilon_c>\mu\); for a uniform core,
the clock's logarithmic gradient length is
\(L_N=2\mu r/[3(\epsilon_c-\mu)]\). These are consequences of the
rest-mass assignment, not a derivation of it. They cannot extend with
constant \(\epsilon_c>\mu\) to a regular positive-clock center:
the required current per particle diverges there.

\paragraph{2. Exclusion and reversal.}
Sixteen spin--valley states per continuum momentum give
\(n=8k_F^3/(3\pi^2)\) only for a specified completely filled set of
eight disjoint Fermi balls. Count alone supplies a volume bound, not
a compression radius. The advection velocity \(v\cos(2ak)\) reverses
at \(|ak|=\pi/4\), while its current symbol is
\(q=\sin(2ak)/(2a)\). An inversion-symmetric filling has \(J=0\);
for those balls filled to \(ak_F=\pi/4\), its mean bulk velocity
is \(24v/\pi^3<0\). Reversal therefore does not halt that filled core.

\paragraph{3. Spectrum and escape.}
On a uniform applied-field patch (now denoted by \(N,v\)), a held
axial/radial operator with an infinite free lead has upper local band
\[
 \omega(z)=\frac Na[\sqrt{\mu^2+\sin^2z}-|w|\sin z\cos z],\qquad
 b=\sqrt{m^2+a^{-2}}.
\]
The positive asymptotic propagating band is \((m,b)\); a local
outward velocity, or a weight assigned to this interval, supplies
no transmission or infinity-escape fraction. The full operator also
has a negative continuum \((-b,-m)\): splitting only at zero is
insufficient to classify all negative-frequency content. Spectral
weights require the actual state and the same complete operator.
If \emph{each} local same-field contribution obeys
\(\varepsilon+wq=m\), then \(\omega=Nm<m\) wherever \(N<1\);
the mean mass/count ansatz alone does not impose this on each particle.
An additional fixed density and uniformly bounded momentum distribution give only a
local \(O(\chi^{1/3}/[N(g\mu)^{1/6}])\) propagating-band window,
at fixed \(\mu\), with \(N\) bounded away from zero;
it is not an escaping share.

\paragraph{4. Turning.}
For radial continuum motion in a held vacuum exterior, a positive
\(0<\omega<m\) has outer turning radius
\(r_{\rm turn}=r_s/[1-(\omega/m)^2]\), provided the turning momentum
is in the valley and the radius lies in that exterior. This is no
universal bound radius. Near the upper lattice band edge,
\(\omega>b\), the axial weak-flow envelope instead gives
\(r_{\rm turn}\sim r_s/[2(\omega/b-1)]\). These are turning
comparators, not sharp support boundaries or bound-state counts.

\paragraph{5. Annulus and root motion.}
If the tangential evolution row holds for nonnegative band-diagonal
matter, its root rate is
\[
 \mathcal M_t=8\pi Nr^2\int_p f\frac{(\varepsilon-q_r)^2}{\varepsilon}>0.
\]
Thus a simple outer root cannot recede under those premises, even
at \(ak=\pi/4\). Native outward number velocity there defeats an
extrapolation of continuum no-outflow; the finite-zone history need
not satisfy this evolution row. A moving spherical cut reads
\(F-4\pi R^2\dot R\,n(R)\);
root recession's sweep is distinct from material flux.

\paragraph{6. Thermal window.}
Only with a matched exponential ray map, regular incoming vacuum
and transmission on the vacuum exterior, \(\kappa=1/(2r_s)\), can the light channel have
\(L=\mathcal L/r_s^2\), \(\dot S_{\rm cg}=\mathcal S/r_s\).
The forming species instead has \(m/T_H=4\pi\mu/\chi\).
For thermal frequencies \(\omega r_s=O(1)\), the stated material
peeling comparison ends at
\[
\begin{aligned}
 T_d=\tfrac23r_s\log\chi^{-1}+O(r_s),\qquad
 u_d=2T_d+O(r_s),\\
 \frac{E_{\rm win}}E=\tfrac83\frac G{a^2}\mathcal L\chi^2\log\chi^{-1}
                      +O(\chi^2).
\end{aligned}
\]
Here \(T_d\) is the emission Schwarzschild clock and \(u_d\) the
retarded arrival window, not a tabulated flat-slice collar clock.
The coarse entropy is only \(O(\log\chi^{-1})\), not an area-sized
Page rise or a fine-grained entropy prediction.

\paragraph{7. After the window.}
A complete stationary one-ended operator with incoming Killing
vacuum has zero stationary spontaneous flux, exactly. Formed
excitations can still drain after \(T_d\), without a universal
deadline; an interior held fixed alone is not the complete premise.
For a bouncing interior no power of \(\chi\) or late coefficient
is fixed here. It requires the time-dependent occupied-to-empty
matrix elements, their frequency content and outward transfer.
Neither a lattice-scale bounce nor the low-momentum stencil error
supplies them.

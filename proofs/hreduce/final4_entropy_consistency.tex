\begin{center}\textbf{Which entropy is tied to the Newton coefficient?}\end{center}

\paragraph{Consistency target.}
Use $\hbar=c=k_B=1$ and $\ell_P^2=G$.
$G$ is defined by the Cavendish measurement; the single condition
$k_a(1)=(2\pi G)^{-1}$ constrains the inventory of $H_*$ at its chosen
spacing. Suppose the physical exterior is the locked Schwarzschild
exterior, its regular quantum state has the surface-gravity temperature,
and the quasistationary first law holds at fixed charges and fixed $G$.
Then, with energy $E$ read at infinity,
\[
 R_H=2GE,\quad \mathcal A=16\pi G^2E^2,\quad
 T_H=\frac1{8\pi GE},\qquad
 dS=\frac{dE}{T_H}=d\!\left(\frac{\mathcal A}{4G}\right).
\]
Thus $S=\mathcal A/(4G)+S_0$ on that family. An additive constant
cannot repair an incorrect leading area coefficient. This is a
consistency requirement conditional on the stated exterior,
temperature and thermodynamic regime, not a temperature calculation
for the microscopic flow law. It contains no entropy calibration or
choice of spacing in Planck units.

\paragraph{The precise regulated replica identity.}
Fix a finite regulator, a positive Hilbert space and a specified
factorizing regional algebra. For a state with finite entropy and reduced density
matrix $\rho_A$, cyclic gluing of the \emph{same} transfer operator
defines $Z_a(n)/Z_a(1)^n=\Tr\rho_A^n$. Writing $W_a=-\log Z_a$ gives
the exact spectral identity
\begin{equation}
 S_A=\left.\partial_n[W_a(n)-nW_a(1)]\right|_{n=1}.
 \label{eq:ec-replica}
\end{equation}
It holds by diagonalizing $\rho_A$; zero eigenvalues use $0\log0=0$.
It is an identity about the chosen algebra, fields, state and cutoff,
not an identity for an arbitrary stiffness functional.

To identify its leading local area term with Newton renormalization,
require that the same gluing has a covariant effective-action
description at the retained scale, with one curvature term
\[
 W_{a,\mathrm{loc}}[g]=-Z_R(a)\int\sqrt g\,R+\cdots,
 \qquad \delta(1/G)_a=16\pi Z_R(a),
\]
and no unaccounted independent seam term, boundary term, edge sector
or curvature-contact contribution. Smooth the cone compatibly with
the same regulator and take its controlled local-area limit.
For a planar or locally stationary bifurcation cut,
\[
 \int\sqrt{g_n}R_n-n\int\sqrt{g_1}R_1
        =4\pi(1-n)\mathcal A+O((n-1)^2).
\]
Equation~\eqref{eq:ec-replica} then gives
\begin{equation}
 S_{\mathrm{area},a}=4\pi Z_R(a)\mathcal A
       =\frac{\mathcal A}{4}\,\delta(1/G)_a.
 \label{eq:ec-same-fields}
\end{equation}
This is the conditional lattice version: the regulated replica
identity is exact, while identifying its area response with the
bulk curvature coefficient requires the stated effective-action
and cut-matching properties. A spatial lattice or a uniform
relativistic dispersion alone does not establish them. Higher
curvature and nonlocal entropy terms are outside the area identity.
For nonminimal couplings or a constrained algebra the appropriate
contact and edge contributions must be included explicitly; they
cannot silently be called ordinary site entropy.

If the entire Newton coefficient is induced by these same fields
with this cutoff, \eqref{eq:ec-same-fields} is
$\mathcal A/(4G)$. If there is an independent gravitational term,
its area contribution must be counted as well; the field entropy
alone is not generally the entire result. The conical curvature
calculation and the exterior first-law calculation agree on the
target coefficient under their respective stated premises.

\paragraph{The polar coefficient is a different known object.}
For the actual scalar-source law,
\[
 W_0=\sum_s\eta_s\Tr[N\psi(E_{s1}-E_{s0})],\qquad
 k_a(1)\longrightarrow\frac{3J}{\pi^2},\qquad
 J=\sum_s\eta_sm_s^2\log(m_s^2/\mu^2)>0.
\]
The strictly positive nondegenerate rectangle has
$\eta=(+,-,-,+)$ and $\sum\eta=\sum\eta m_s^2=0$.
This coefficient is the gradient splitting between chosen fermion
and boson polar channels. It is not obtained by integrating all
physical packets with minimal covariant metric coupling.

There is a finite-lattice test independent of continuum terminology.
At homogeneous unit fields the positive and reversed fermion packets
have Hamiltonians related by the site-local Dirac-block swap
$\tau_1\otimes I$. This unitary factorizes across the site cut, so
the two orientations have identical cut spectra and entropy.
Reversing every orientation instead sends the scalar-source vacuum
functional and its stiffness to their negatives. The same-field
site entropy is unchanged. Thus the polar stiffness and the native
cut entropy cannot obey a universal orientation-independent
same-field identity across this explicit positive-norm family.

The bosons make the distinction sharper. Each native oscillator
block is $b^\dagger B b+\tfrac12\Tr B$. Its empty state is the
product annihilated by every site $b_x$, even when $B$ is nonlocal;
its native-site cut entropy is zero. The assigned zero-point term
still contributes to the polar gradient response. A covariant
scalar-field cut uses a different local field algebra and coupling;
it cannot be substituted for these oscillators by counting their
components.

The coefficient comparison is explicit. For one minimally coupled
4-component Dirac cone, the spin connection and $R/4$ potential in
its square give $\operatorname{tr}_4a_1=-R/3$. Consequently
\[
 S_D/\mathcal A=\frac1{24\pi}\int_0^\infty\frac{dt}{t^2}e^{-m^2t},
 \qquad \delta(1/G)_D=\frac1{6\pi}
                   \int_0^\infty\frac{dt}{t^2}e^{-m^2t},
\]
with the same regulator in both expressions. Grouping the rectangle
\emph{before} removing the regulator replaces the integral by $J$.
The 8 sine valleys then give
\begin{equation}
 \lim_{a\to0}\frac{S_{\rm gr}(a)}{\mathcal A}=\frac{J}{3\pi},\qquad
 k_{\mathrm{Dirac,gr}}:=\frac{\delta(1/G)_{\mathrm{Dirac,gr}}}{2\pi}
       =\frac{2J}{3\pi^2},\qquad
 \frac{k_{\mathrm{Dirac,gr}}}{k_{*,\mathrm{cont}}}=\frac29.
 \label{eq:ec-coefficients}
\end{equation}
The native cut coefficient is also established without a cone input
by its exact half-chain Toeplitz spectrum and shifted transverse
measure. The covariant Dirac check therefore identifies the
coefficient paired with that formal graded entropy; it does not
identify it with the polar coefficient. This is a factor $9/2$
discrepancy, not a free normalization to be fitted.

\paragraph{An analytic lower bound on the unsigned cut.}
Use $N=\psi=1$, the planar $[100]$ native site cut, one interface,
and the thermodynamic limits of the graded-cut proof, adding all
4 packet entropies positively. Write $C_A=P_A P^-P_A$ for one
packet's restricted sea projector. The elementary binary-entropy
inequality $h(\lambda)\geq2\lambda(1-\lambda)$ gives
\[
 S_s\geq2\Tr C_A(1-C_A)
 =2\sum_{x\in A,\,y\notin A}\|P^-_{xy}\|_F^2.
\]
Retain just the nearest-neighbor bond crossing each boundary cell.
For $\mu=a m_s\leq1/2$, the full sine-zone projector gives
\[
 P^-_{\boldsymbol e_1}=-\frac{i\alpha_1}{2}c_m,\qquad
 c_m=\int_{[-\pi,\pi]^3}\frac{d^3\theta}{(2\pi)^3}
 \frac{\sin^2\theta_1}{\sqrt{\sum_j\sin^2\theta_j+\mu^2}}
 \geq\frac1{\sqrt{13}}.
\]
Here $\operatorname{tr}_4\alpha_1^\dagger\alpha_1=4$, so
$\|P^-_{\boldsymbol e_1}\|_F^2=c_m^2\geq1/13$.
The denominator is at most $\sqrt{13}/2$ and the zone average
of $\sin^2\theta_1$ is $1/2$. Thus
\begin{equation}
 \eta_+:=a^2\frac{S_+}{\mathcal A}\geq\frac8{13},\qquad
 \frac{S_+}{\mathcal A/(4G)}\geq
 \frac{32}{13}\left(\frac{\ell_P}{a}\right)^2>10^4
 \quad\begin{matrix}a/\ell_P\leq0.0059,\\a m_{\max}\leq1/2.\end{matrix}
 \label{eq:ec-overshoot}
\end{equation}
The full zone and the 4-component Frobenius norm already count the
8 valleys and internal multiplicities; no extra valley factor is
inserted. Reversing a packet orientation preserves the bond norm.

The independent exact half-chain spectrum gives the same large
discrepancy with a weaker bound. Its transverse parameter satisfies
$t^2=\sin^2\theta_2+\sin^2\theta_3+a^2m_s^2\leq9/4$.
Put $k=(1+t^2)^{-1/2}$, $k'=\sqrt{1-k^2}$ and
$\varepsilon=\pi K(k')/K(k)$. The elementary elliptic-integral
bounds $K(k)\geq\pi/2$ and $K(k')\leq\pi/(2k)$ give
$\varepsilon\leq C:=\pi\sqrt{13}/2<6$.
For $F(v)=\log(1+e^{-v})+v/(1+e^v)$,
$F(v)\geq(1+v)e^{-v}/2$ and $F$ decreases. Retaining only the
first CTM level, the half-chain factor $2$ and the transverse
Dirac factor $2$ therefore give
\[
 \eta_+\geq16F(C)\geq8(1+C)e^{-C}>\frac7{64}.
\]
The last bound uses $C<6$ and $e^6<512$. All 8 sine valleys and
one interface are already counted by this exact spectral formula.
It independently yields a ratio greater than $10^4$ under the same
two scale conditions. These are native planar-cut bounds, not
microcanonical or horizon-state theorems.

\paragraph{Positive-state multiplicities cannot be graded.}
The protected reference is a tensor product over the physical
packets, so at the native site cut
\[
 \rho_A=\bigotimes_s\rho_{A,s},\qquad
 S(\rho_A)=\sum_sS(\rho_{A,s}),
\]
with no orientation signs; the boson factors have zero entropy.
The formal replica ratio $\prod_s(\Tr\rho_{A,s}^n)^{\eta_s}$
generates $\sum_s\eta_sS_s$, but is not the replica trace of that
positive product state. Pauli--Villars subtraction can define an
effective-action regulator; it does not turn actual positive-norm
packets into negative multiplicities.

For a specified invariant physical sector with orthogonal projector
$P_{\rm phys}$ and self-adjoint boundary charge $Q$,
$[P_{\rm phys},Q]=0$, its finite microcanonical multiplicity is
\[
 \Omega(E,\delta E)=
 \Tr\bigl(P_{\rm phys}\,\mathbf1_{[E,E+\delta E]}(Q)\bigr)
       \in\{0,1,2,\ldots\}.
\]
Every admitted state contributes $+1$, including states involving
near-surface modes. A signed trace is an index-like quantity, not
this dimension; adding the sea modes cannot change that fact.
Neither this positivity nor the vacuum cut proves area scaling of
$\log\Omega$. A unique gapped entangled vacuum already has one
global ground state and nonzero regional entropy. For the dressed
count, an $O(E\log E)$ upper bound requires the stated common-Fock
restriction, polynomial mode count and region-uniform dressed gap;
that uniform gap is not supplied by positivity alone.

Likewise, a temperature divided by an entropy ``overshoot'' follows
only if the \emph{actual thermodynamic} entropy is
$S(E)=r\mathcal A(E)/(4G)$ with $r$ independent of $E$ at fixed
$G$ and cutoff, and with the relevant work terms absent:
then $T^{-1}=dS/dE=r/T_H$. A vacuum cut ratio by itself does not
establish that entropy or detailed-balance temperature.
For an energy-dependent ratio the derivative instead gives
$T^{-1}=[r+(E/2)r']/T_H$, at fixed $G$ and cutoff.

\paragraph{Diagnosis and remaining identification.}
The huge unsigned native-cut excess under the displayed conditions is a
failed identification with the required first-law entropy, not an
acceptable entropy of a theory claimed to have that gravity limit.
The graded diagnostic cancels its cutoff terms but gives $2/9$ of
the matched area density in the continuum; it is also not a count
of this positive Hilbert space. Neither result supplies the physical
entropy required by the exterior consistency test. A consistent
realization must identify its physical regional algebra and boundary
state count, and show that their area response is paired with the
same effective-action coefficient measured by Cavendish. The known
static polar splitting does not establish this pairing. A change of
algebra, covariant completion or minimal model change would need
that demonstration with the same fields and cutoff, not an entropy
normalization. Tens-of-percent agreement is the stated tolerance;
an exact rational coefficient is not required, while an orders-of-
magnitude excess cannot be accepted.
The physical boundary-charge entropy, its relation to the native
cut, and the temperature of a self-consistent $H_*$ configuration
are not supplied by these results. The established conditional
identity and the explicit failed native-cut identification therefore
do not constitute a consistent black-object thermodynamics.

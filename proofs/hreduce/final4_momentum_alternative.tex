\subsection*{A minimal flow replacement: necessary changes}
Quantum mechanics is the starting level; H2 is a separate declared
hypothesis.  Use $\hbar=c=1$.
A spatial Gauss equation does not itself specify a
readout's equal-time algebra or a change of time slicing.

For the chosen flat free sine Hamiltonian
$h(\mathbf p)=\beta m+\sum_i\alpha_i\sin(ap_i)/a$, its symmetrically
allocated energy dipole fixes the bulk current:
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 Q_i&=i[h,\tfrac12\{X_i,h\}]
       =\frac{[X_i,h^2]}{2i}\\
    &=\tfrac12\partial_{p_i}(h^2)
       =\frac{\sin(2ap_i)}{2a}\,I .
 \end{aligned}
\]
Independently, the band energy-velocity current
$\{h,\partial_{p_i}h\}/2$ gives the same scalar operator by the
Clifford anticommutators.
[X: energy-dipole commutator; Clifford band-current identity.]
Here $X_i=i\partial_{p_i}$ is the infinite-lattice bulk position
operator, not a periodic position matrix.
Using this current removes a separate stencil choice at this free
scope.  It does not identify the completed interacting energy
current with a conserved momentum or supply a momentum flux.

\emph{Changing the stencil alone cannot supply spatial gauge symmetry.}

Consider any positive prescribed-background six-component metric
$\gamma$, scalar clock $N$, volume ruler $\psi$ and complete static
polar vacuum $W$, with
\[
 \psi=(\det\gamma)^{1/12},\qquad
 W=\langle N\psi,X[\gamma]\rangle .
\]
Here $X_x$ is the full packet-weighted spin trace of
$(|A^\dagger|-|A|)_{xx}$.
The finite-matrix singular-value identity gives $\sum_xX_x=0$,
including the collar; normality gives $X=0$ at the flat reference.
For $\gamma=I+f$, $N=1+n$, and $X=Rf+O(f^2)$,
\[
 W^{(2)}=\langle n+\ell f,Rf\rangle,
 \qquad \ell f=\tfrac1{12}\operatorname{tr}f .
\]
At a nonzero Fourier mode of a translation-invariant flat regulator,
write $r=R(q)\ne0$.  Require the symmetry to map the flat vacuum
to stationary vacua.  Its displacement is then a full
vacuum-Hessian null vector and obeys
\[
 r\,\delta f=0,\qquad
 r^\dagger(\delta n+\ell\delta f)+\ell^\dagger r\,\delta f=0,
\]
so $\delta n+\ell\delta f=0$.
An ordinary spatial relabeling keeps a uniform scalar clock fixed
but has $\delta f_{ij}=i(k_i\xi_j+k_j\xi_i)$, with $k_i(q)$ the
chosen nonzero difference symbol, tending to $q_i$ in the continuum.
Its longitudinal part violates this necessary condition.
Nonzero long-wavelength scalar stiffness supplies $r\ne0$;
no particular tensor hopping placement was used.

An independent continuum check assumes the full compact spatial Lie
action.  At $N=\psi^{-1}$, the volume weight gives
$\delta(N\psi)=(\nabla\cdot\xi)/6$, whereas $\int\delta X=0$.
Consequently
\[
 \delta W=\tfrac16\int X\,\nabla\cdot\xi
          =-\tfrac16\int\xi\cdot\nabla X .
\]
Spatial Ward invariance would force $X$ constant, hence zero on a
periodic regulator or with flat asymptotic data, contradicting the
nonzero scalar response.
[X: full Hessian factorization; constant-allocation Lie variation,
for the spatial Ward exclusion.]
Thus changes solely inside $A$ cannot achieve that symmetry.
One must change a stated vacuum/allocation/clock premise, or forgo
this symmetry; H2 alone does not demand it.

\emph{A spatial gauge action would still not select a flat slice.}
For the mass-locked two-derivative continuum exterior
$\psi=1+A/r$, $A>0$,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{Ric}_{rr}[\psi^4\delta]
       &=-\frac{4A}{\psi^2r^3}\ne0,\\
 \frac{d(r\psi^2)}{d\ell}&=\frac{r-A}{r+A},
 \qquad d\ell=\psi^2\,dr .
 \end{aligned}
\]
The Ricci tensor and the areal-radius slope both exclude a
Euclidean spatial metric on $r>A$.
[X: conformal Ricci tensor; invariant areal-radius slope.]
A spatial coordinate change preserves this curvature.
A flat-ruler flow description therefore requires a different time
foliation with its own consistent temporal law, or an explicitly
restricted physical configuration space.

\emph{A concrete small alternative fails as a standalone closure.}
Choose a real auxiliary flow $v$ on the fixed lattice, coupled to
the energy current above, and a quadratic field cost.
At isotropic continuum derivative order its fixed-state energy
may be chosen as
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathcal F[v]&=E_0+\langle v,j\rangle\\
 &\quad+\tfrac12\int
 \left[\mu^2|v|^2+\kappa_T|\nabla\times v|^2
                 +\kappa_L(\nabla\cdot v)^2\right],
 \end{aligned}
\]
with $\mu,\kappa_T,\kappa_L\ge0$ and prescribed compact source
$j$ independent of the varied $v$.
This is an added energy law; its coefficients are not derived
from $H_*$.
Its Euler equation is
\[
 \mu^2v+\kappa_T\nabla\times(\nabla\times v)
       -\kappa_L\nabla(\nabla\cdot v)=-j .
\]
It supplies a constitutive equation, not a gauge generator or a
derivation of H2's momentum constraint.
Retaining the protected uniform-flow vacuum cancellation requires
$\mu=0$ in this added vacuum cost.
In a source-free spherical exterior, $v=w(r)\hat r$ gives
\[
 \mu^2w-\kappa_L\left(w''+\frac{2w'}r-\frac{2w}{r^2}\right)=0 .
\]
For $\mu=0$, $\kappa_L>0$, its solutions are $w=c_1r+c_2/r^2$;
decay removes $c_1$.  For $\mu>0$, $\kappa_L>0$, the decaying
tail is screened.
Independently, the longitudinal Green function
$(\mu^2+\kappa_Lq^2)^{-1}$ gives a Coulomb or Yukawa potential;
its radial gradient gives these same decaying tails.
[X: radial Euler equation; longitudinal Green function.]
Neither tail supplies the flat-ruler, unit-clock exterior
comparator $w^2=2GE/r$, where $G$ is defined solely by the
Cavendish measurement.
If $\mu=\kappa_L=0$, this row leaves every radial profile
undetermined; if $\kappa_L=0<\mu$, it gives $w=0$ outside.
These are continuum, prescribed-source statements, not exact
radial identities of a cubic lattice.

A coupled lapse equation or a self-consistent induced source
$j[v]$ can change that conclusion.  Neither is supplied by this
quadratic test.  No replacement of all 3 flow declarations is
established: a model retaining ordinary spatial gauge symmetry
must change a premise of the polar theorem, and a physical
flat-ruler foliation needs a temporal construction.
[V: momentum alternative, microscopic vacuum and temporal law.]

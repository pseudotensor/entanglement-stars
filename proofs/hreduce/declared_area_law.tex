\noindent\textbf{[R] The free sea's cut coefficient.}
Put $\mu=|m|a$ and use nats.  Restrict the occupied projector of
$a h_0(\mathbf k)=\sum_i\alpha_i\sin k_i+\mu\beta$ to the actual
three-dimensional set of sites $A$:
$C_A=\Pi_A(1-h_0/|h_0|)\Pi_A/2$ and
$S_A=\operatorname{tr}h(C_A)$, where
$h(c)=-c\log c-(1-c)\log(1-c)$.
For one planar boundary, transverse Fourier transformation gives exactly
\[
 \eta_{100}(\mu)=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\!\frac{d k_y\,d k_z}{(2\pi)^2}
 s_4\!\left(\sqrt{\mu^2+\sin^2 k_y+\sin^2 k_z}\right),\quad
 s_4(M)=4\sum_{j\geq0}h_F((2j+1)\epsilon_M),
\]
where $h_F(z)=\log(1+e^{-z})+z/(e^z+1)$ and
$\epsilon_M=\pi K(M^2/(1+M^2))/K(1/(1+M^2))$; $K$ uses the
elliptic parameter.  The Calabrese--Cardy anchor is
$s_4(M)=\tfrac23\log(1/M)+O(1)$: two spin copies and two longitudinal
valleys, already counted.  For each fixed primitive plane in physical-area units,
\[
 \eta_{\mathbf n}(\mu)=\eta_{\mathbf n}(0)
       +\frac{\mu^2}{3\pi}\log\mu^2+O(\mu^2).
\]
For $[100]$ the remainder is $b\mu^2+O(\mu^4\log\mu)$.

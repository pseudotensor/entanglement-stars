\paragraph{Cone and isotropy [X].}
At a valley $p_i=\nu_i\pi/a+k_i$, $\nu_i\in\{0,1\}$, the declared
sine Hamiltonian has
\[
 E^2=m^2+k^2-{a^2\over3}\sum_i k_i^4+O(a^4k^6),\qquad
 Q_i=k_i-{2a^2\over3}k_i^3+O(a^4k_i^5).
\]
The Clifford matrices acquire valley signs; the dispersion and the flow's
unit slope do not.  For uniform $N>0,v$,
$\omega_\pm=v\cdot Q\pm NE$, so all 8 valleys approach the same
mass shell $(\omega-v\cdot k)^2=N^2(m^2+k^2)$.
The massless rays therefore read
$ds^2=-N^2dt^2+|dx-vdt|^2$ at long wavelengths.
At $v=0,N=1$, writing $k=k\boldsymbol n$ and
$A_4(\boldsymbol n)=\sum_i n_i^4$, their phase and radial group speeds are
\[
 {E\over k}=1-{(ak)^2\over6}A_4+O((ak)^4),\qquad
 \boldsymbol n\cdot\nabla_k E
 =1-{(ak)^2\over2}A_4+O((ak)^4).
\]
Here $A_4=1,1/2,1/3$ along $[100],[110],[111]$.
This proves long-wavelength isotropy and fixes its leading cubic correction;
the cone is the continuum propagation cone, not a strict support boundary
for a lattice propagator.

\paragraph{3. Exact Gaussian entropy and its mixed-state qualification.}
For a native, gauge-invariant quasifree light state define
\begin{equation}
 C_{ij}(T)=\Tr(\rho_\ell c_j^\dagger c_i),\quad
 0\leq C\leq1,\quad
 C_A=P_A C P_A\big|_{\mathfrak h_A},\quad
 G_{A\bar A}=P_A C P_{\bar A},\quad
 s(t)=-t\log t-(1-t)\log(1-t).
 \tag{XP3}
\end{equation}
Here and below $0\log0=0$ and entropies use natural logarithms.
Diagonalizing $C_A$ gives independent Bernoulli occupations,
and hence
\begin{equation}
 S_A=\tr s(C_A),\quad S_{\bar A}=\tr s(C_{\bar A}),\quad
 S_\ell=\tr s(C),\quad
 {\cal I}(A:\bar A)=S_A+S_{\bar A}-S_\ell .
 \tag{XP4}
\end{equation}
Equivalently the restricted Gaussian density is the normalized
exponential of a quadratic number operator with one-mode
occupation eigenvalues equal to those of $C_A$.  This derivation
does not use the source allocation or a continuum limit.

If $C^2=C$, the full light state is a pure Slater state and
$S_A=S_{\bar A}={\cal I}/2$ is its mode entanglement entropy.
The cosine--sine decomposition of its occupied subspace gives
independent occupied modes
$\sqrt{\nu_j}a_j^\dagger+\sqrt{1-\nu_j}b_j^\dagger$ across the cut,
and the same entropy $\sum_j s(\nu_j)$.
For a pure state of fixed total native number the further
charge-resolved decomposition is
\[
 S_A=H(\{p_n\})+\sum_n p_n S(\rho_{A,n}),\qquad
 p_n=\Tr(\rho_A\Pi_{A,n}).
\]
Reporting the within-sector average instead of the full mode
entropy is an additional declared local-charge readout convention;
the two quantities must not be conflated.

The actual fractional-load Gaussian preparation is
$C(T_0)=P_-+\lambda P_g$, with $0\leq\lambda\leq1$,
$P_-P_g=0$ and
$\operatorname{rank}P_g=2$.  Consequently, exactly,
\begin{equation}
 S_\ell(T)=2s(\lambda),\qquad
 \tr\{C(T)(1-C(T))\}=2\lambda(1-\lambda).
 \tag{XP5}
\end{equation}
The native unitary evolution preserves these values even when
its one-body generator depends self-consistently on the state.
Thus $0<\lambda<1$ is a mixed preparation: $S_A$ alone is not
interior--exterior entanglement and ${\cal I}$ is total correlation,
not an entanglement measure.  A pure-light entanglement curve
requires a pure initial light state and dynamics preserving its
purity.  Factorized packet dynamics with classical solved fields
has this property when the packets start in a product state.
It cannot be assumed after tracing an initially correlated packet.

For a mixed light state an exact possible mode-entanglement
definition is the entanglement of formation in the declared
occupation tensor convention,
$E_F(\rho_{A\bar A})=\inf_{\rho=\sum_jp_j|\varphi_j\rangle
\langle\varphi_j|}\sum_jp_jS(\rho_{A,j})$.
This requires the joint density matrix and an optimization;
(XP4) is not a formula for it.  Restricting the decomposition
or local operations by superselection defines a different
operational quantity and must be stated.  The finite-parent
Gaussian algorithm reports (XP4)--(XP5) without such an
unperformed mixed-state entanglement claim.

\paragraph{4. Cross-surface correlations and an exact purity identity.}
The canonical equal-time two-point function across the surface
is precisely $G_{A\bar A}$.  For disjoint native modes $i,j$,
Wick contraction gives
\begin{equation}
 \langle n_i n_j\rangle_c=-|C_{ij}|^2,\qquad
 \Cov(N_A,N_{\bar A})=-\|G_{A\bar A}\|_{\rm HS}^2,\qquad
 C_A(1-C_A)=G_{A\bar A}G_{A\bar A}^\dagger
             +P_A(C-C^2)P_A .
 \tag{XP6}
\end{equation}
The last identity follows independently by multiplying the
two-by-two block matrix $C$; it isolates mixedness from
cross-surface correlation.  For a pure Slater state its last
term is zero.  In that case the singular values $g_j$ of
$G_{A\bar A}$ obey $g_j^2=\nu_j(1-\nu_j)$, and
\[
 S_A=\sum_j s\!\left(\frac{1-\sqrt{1-4g_j^2}}2\right).
\]
Zero singular values contribute zero.  This is a useful independent
readback of the pure Gaussian entropy, not a formula for a mixed
or non-Gaussian state.

For site fields normalized in coordinate volume,
$\Psi_\ell(x)=a^{-3/2}c_x$, the reported spinor kernel is
$a^{-3}C_{x\alpha,y\beta}$.  Proper-volume normalization instead
adds $\psi_x^{-3}\psi_y^{-3}$ to this kernel.
Entropy always uses the canonical mode matrix (XP3), not that
rescaled kernel with an unchanged counting measure.
For normalized canonical detector modes $f,g$, the two-point
readout is $f^\dagger Cg$.  Local spin-basis changes transform
the two endpoints and leave singular-value or Hilbert--Schmidt
readouts invariant.

Along the same Gaussian history let ${\cal U}(T,T')$ solve
$i\partial_T{\cal U}=h[N(T),\psi(T)]{\cal U}$.  With no extra
factor of $i$ in the Wightman convention, the unequal-time kernels are
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 G^<(T,T')&={\cal U}(T,T')C(T'),&
 G^>(T,T')&={\cal U}(T,T')(1-C(T')),\\
 G^<_{A\bar A}(T,T')&=P_{A(T)}G^<(T,T')P_{\bar A(T')},&
 G^<+G^>&={\cal U}(T,T').
 \end{aligned}\tag{XP7}
\end{equation}
For example $G^<_{ij}(T,T')=\langle c_j^\dagger(T')c_i(T)\rangle$.
The two-time prescription holds the solved background history
fixed in these operator insertions; it does not solve new fields
for a measurement-conditioned state after each insertion.
Such a conditional measurement law is not determined by
the history-dependent mean-state evolution alone.
The anticommutator in the last line checks the common evolution
and normalization.  A polar calculation uses both endpoint
frames, $W^\dagger(T)G^{\lessgtr}(T,T')W(T')$, and the
transported projectors $W^\dagger(T)P_AW(T)$.
Keeping a site-diagonal cut after this nonlocal frame change
defines a different algebra.  Soft spatial masks are not
orthogonal subsystem projectors.
Only for disjoint sets at equal time does the projected
anticommutator vanish and give $G^>_{A\bar A}=-G^<_{A\bar A}$.

\paragraph{5. Dynamics and motion of the cut are distinct.}
Between site-crossing times, $P_A$ is constant and
$\dot C=-i[h,C]$.  With every spectral or nonlocal term crossing
the cut retained,
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \dot C_A&=-i\{[h_{AA},C_A]+h_{A\bar A}C_{\bar A A}
                              -C_{A\bar A}h_{\bar A A}\},\\
 \frac{d}{dT}\langle N_A\rangle
     &=2\,\operatorname{Im}\tr(h_{A\bar A}C_{\bar A A}),\\
 \dot S_A&=-i\,\tr\!\left[
       \log\frac{1-C_A}{C_A}
       (h_{A\bar A}C_{\bar A A}-C_{A\bar A}h_{\bar A A})
                                      \right].
 \end{aligned}\tag{XP8}
\end{equation}
The internal commutator drops out of the last line because the
logarithm commutes with $C_A$.  Use that line when the active
eigenvalues stay away from zero and one, or first replace the
logarithm by $\log[(1-C_A+\epsilon)/(C_A+\epsilon)]$ and control
the limit.  The continuous entropy formula (XP4) remains valid
at pure eigenvalues; a finite entropy derivative is not assumed.

At a hard-cut jump $A_-\to A_+$ the state has no jump.
Record separately
\begin{equation}
 \Delta_{\rm cut}S=S_{A_+}(C(T_*))-S_{A_-}(C(T_*)),\qquad
 \Delta_{\rm cut}N=\tr\{(P_{A_+}-P_{A_-})C(T_*)\}.
 \tag{XP9}
\end{equation}
These changes reassign modes; they are not pair production or
an entropy source.  A sampled calculation must either locate $T_*$
with controlled interpolation of this same history, or use the
exact endpoint decomposition
\[
 \begin{split}
 S_{A_{n+1}}(C_{n+1})-S_{A_n}(C_n)
 ={}&[S_{A_n}(C_{n+1})-S_{A_n}(C_n)]\\
   &+[S_{A_{n+1}}(C_{n+1})-S_{A_n}(C_{n+1})].
 \end{split}
\]
The second term is endpoint reassignment, not a located physical
crossing.  For number the two terms are respectively
$\tr[P_{A_n}(C_{n+1}-C_n)]$ and
$\tr[(P_{A_{n+1}}-P_{A_n})C_{n+1}]$.
An endpoint decomposition alone does not resolve intermediate
multiple crossings.  For a smooth family of orthonormal detector
isometries $Q_A(T)$ of fixed rank, the corresponding alternative
readout has
$\dot C_A=\dot Q_A^\dagger C Q_A+Q_A^\dagger\dot C Q_A+
Q_A^\dagger C\dot Q_A$.  Its first and last terms are retained.
It is not obtained by treating a soft mask as a partial trace.

A spatial exterior at each time is not automatically a nested
algebra of accumulated outgoing radiation.  Such a radiation
readout needs separately declared orthonormal outgoing detector
modes or a detector model.  Reusing the same mode at successive
times does not create independent tensor factors, and entropies
of successive samples cannot be added.  The handoff curve here
is the declared spatial-cut entropy/correlation curve.

\paragraph{6. What survives for the actual dressed light state.}
For the complete interacting law use
$\rho_A=\Tr_{\bar A}\rho_\ell$ in the declared fermionic convention,
$S_A=-\Tr\rho_A\log\rho_A$ and
$C_{ij}=\Tr\rho_\ell c_j^\dagger c_i$.
The evolution of $C$ is
$\dot C_{ij}=i\Tr\rho_\ell[H_\ell,c_j^\dagger c_i]$,
with the corresponding full-state expectations if other sectors
are not factorized.  It is not a closed one-body equation.
Unequal-time kernels use the actual many-body Heisenberg
operators and their ordered insertions.  There is no one-body
propagator replacement of (XP7) for a general interacting state.
A pure interacting light state still has bipartite entropy
$S_A=S_{\bar A}$, but need not be Gaussian or satisfy $C^2=C$.

Let $\rho_A^G$ be the Gaussian state with the same native
$C_A$, and assume charge invariance.  Its logarithm is quadratic,
so $\Tr\rho_A\log\rho_A^G=\Tr\rho_A^G\log\rho_A^G$.  Therefore
\begin{equation}
 \tr s(C_A)-S(\rho_A)
       =D(\rho_A\Vert\rho_A^G)\geq0 .
 \tag{XP10}
\end{equation}
Eigenvalues zero or one impose an exactly empty or occupied mode
on every state with that covariance; restrict to the remaining
support or take the continuous limit.  Thus a covariance-only
entropy is an upper bound for the actual reduced entropy,
with a specified non-Gaussian deficit.  This does not make a
Gaussian mutual information an upper or lower bound on the
interacting mutual information.
Independently, diagonalize $C_A$ by a unitary acting inside $A$.
The single-mode density matrices have occupations $\nu_j$ and
entropies $s(\nu_j)$.  Repeated subadditivity then gives
$S(\rho_A)\leq\sum_j s(\nu_j)$ without a Gaussian-state assumption.
This proves the same upper bound by a different route.
For $i\ne j$ the connected four-point remainder
$\langle n_i n_j\rangle-C_{ii}C_{jj}+|C_{ij}|^2$
is also retained; (XP6)'s Wick covariance formula is not
an interacting identity.

The source amplitudes $F_i=e^{-S}d_i e^S$ have ordinary adjoints
$F_i^\dagger=e^S d_i^\dagger e^{-S}$.
The similarity transform does not in general preserve
$\{F_i,F_j^\dagger\}=\delta_{ij}$.
Consequently a matrix $\langle F_j^\dagger F_i\rangle$ is not
a canonical occupation matrix for (XP3)--(XP4).
The overlapping Walsh masks are likewise not independent
subsystem modes.  Canonical polar modes may be used only
with their exact transformed algebra and connection.
The signed difference $C-P_-[\psi]$ is not a covariance either.
It may be used for an excess two-point signal, but not inside
the entropy function.

At fixed finite $a$ one may additionally report
$S_A-S_A^{\rm ref}$ with
$S_A^{\rm ref}=\tr s(P_A P_-[\psi]P_A)$.
This is a signed, reference- and cut-dependent excess entropy,
not an entanglement measure.  Neither the ultraviolet sea
entropy nor its change is thereby proved to have a universal
continuum limit.

\paragraph{7. Error statements and the finite-spacing scope.}
If $C_A,\widehat C_A$ are positive contractions on $m_A$ modes
and $\|C_A-\widehat C_A\|\leq\epsilon\leq1/2$, the eigenvalue
variation and binary-entropy modulus give
\begin{equation}
 |\tr s(C_A)-\tr s(\widehat C_A)|\leq m_A s(\epsilon),\qquad
 \|P_A(C-\widehat C)P_{\bar A}\|\leq\|C-\widehat C\|.
 \tag{XP11}
\end{equation}
A time-step comparison is not such an enclosure by itself.
Covariance eigenvalues outside $[0,1]$ require an error account;
unrecorded clipping is not a validation.
For actual density matrices with trace distance
$t=\|\rho_A-\widehat\rho_A\|_1/2$ and dimension $d_A=2^{m_A}$,
an elementary bound is
$|S(\rho_A)-S(\widehat\rho_A)|
\leq t\log d_A+(1+t)s(t/(1+t))$.
It follows by writing the positive and negative parts of
$\rho_A-\widehat\rho_A$ as two mixtures with a common state
and applying the entropy-of-mixture bounds.
This explicit dimension cost is not supplied by one-body
covariance accuracy in the interacting problem.

If two spatial cuts differ by $m_D$ modes, successive partial
traces and the entropy triangle inequality give
$|S_A-S_{A'}|\leq m_D\log2$ at the same state.
Report this geometric ambiguity, the site-crossing jumps,
cubic angular variation, and the finite collar separately
from dynamical error.  Native-spacing entropy is a finite
quantity; a near-surface sea contribution may depend strongly
on $a$.  A long-wavelength $(\kappa a)^2$ dispersion estimate
does not automatically bound the full ultraviolet entropy.
Parent enlargement, the same readout algebra and controlled
source/boundary tails are required for an isolated transfer.
No coordinate or polar transformation deletes those conditions.

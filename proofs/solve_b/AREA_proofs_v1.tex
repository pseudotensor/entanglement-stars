\section{Objects, state and cut}
In lattice units $a=1$, write $\mu=ma$ and
\[
 h_0(k)=\alpha\cdot\sin k+\mu\beta,\quad
 E(k)^2=\mu^2+\sum_i\sin^2k_i,\quad
 P_0(k)=\tfrac12(1-h_0/E),\quad q_i(k)=\sin k_i\cos k_i.
\]
The operator whose reference is compared is the actual native operator
\[
 h=\tfrac12\{n,h_0\}+\tfrac12\sum_i\{w_i,Q_i\},\qquad
 n=W_0^\dagger N,\quad w=W_1^\dagger v,\quad Q_i=q_i(D).
\]
All bounds concern these adjoint-allocated fields. The full four-component
Brillouin zone and one-face normalization are retained. Entropy is in nats.
For a pure occupied projection $P$ and a finite spatial cut $A$, put
\[
 T_A(P)=\chi_AP\chi_{A^c},\quad C_A=\chi_AP\chi_A,\quad
 S_A(P)=\Tr h_2(C_A)=\Tr g(|T_A(P)|),
\]
where $h_2(u)=-u\log u-(1-u)\log(1-u)$ and
$g(t)=h_2((1-\sqrt{1-4t^2})/2)$, $0\leq t\leq1/2$.
This follows from $T_AT_A^*=C_A(1-C_A)$.
In particular $g(t)=O(t^2(1+|\log t|))$ and
$|g'(t)|\leq C_\alpha t^\alpha$ for every fixed $0<\alpha<1$.
Require regular cuts with a uniform crossing bound
\[
 N_A(z)=\#\{x\in A:x-z\notin A\}
       \leq C_\partial\mathcal A(1+|z|),\qquad
 \mathcal A=A_{\rm geom}/a^2.
\]
Both entropy differences below compare the \emph{same finite cut}.
They are absolute differences of ordinary entropies, not a nonnegative
reference-subtracted entropy or an evolved physical-state assertion.

\section{Massive rate: global path gap and boundary-local response}
\textbf{Assumed domain.} Fix $\mu>0$. On the infinite cubic parent,
take a norm-continuous path of the displayed actual operators from $h_0$
to $h$, with bounded real fields, $n\geq n_*>0$, and a uniform actual
gap $\sigma(h_s)\cap(-\delta,\delta)=\varnothing$, $\delta>0$.
Their spatial extensions satisfy
$\|\partial^\alpha(n,w)\|_\infty\leq M_\alpha\eps^{|\alpha|}$,
$1\leq|\alpha|\leq2$, uniformly on the path. The occupied projection
is the negative sea continued on this path. These hypotheses are not
deduced from positive mass or a local symbol gap.

\textbf{Proved bound.} For sufficiently small $\eps$,
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 \frac{|S_A(P)-S_A(P_0)|}{\mathcal A}
       \leq C\eps^2(1+|\log\eps|)^4 .}             \tag{1}
\end{equation}
The constant is uniform in the cut radius and $\eps$ at the stated
bounds; it may depend on $\mu,\delta,n_*,M_\alpha$ and the field bounds.
It is not uniform as the mass or actual gap vanishes.

\textbf{Proof.} Use the exponential row-and-column Schur algebra.
Taylor expansion of $[f,K]_{xy}=(f(x)-f(y))K_{xy}$ has an explicitly
summable error $C\|D^2f\|_\infty|x-y|^2\|K_{xy}\|$ for the analytic
flat multipliers. Define
\[
 B_{ij}=-\frac{i h_0q_j\partial_iP_0}{2E^2},\qquad
 P_1=\frac12\sum_{ij}\{\partial_iw_j/n,B_{ij}(D)\}.
\]
The identities $\{h_0,\partial_iP_0\}=0$ and
$[h_0,B_{ij}]=-iq_j\partial_iP_0$ cancel the first native commutator.
The lapse gradient vanishes at first order. Thus $P_1=O_\gamma(\eps)$,
$[h,P_0+P_1]=O_\gamma(\eps^2)$ and
$(P_0+P_1)^2-(P_0+P_1)=O_\gamma(\eps^2)$ in that algebra.
The Riesz projection $Q$ of $P_0+P_1$ near 1 differs from it by
$O_\gamma(\eps^2)$, because its correction factors through the
idempotence defect. Then
$h_d=QhQ+(1-Q)h(1-Q)$ differs from $h$ by $O_\gamma(\eps^2)$.

The actual gap contour gives $\|[P_s,P_0]\|\leq C\eps$.
For two projections, the off-diagonal principal-angle value is
$\sin\theta\cos\theta$. Norm continuity from $P_0$ prevents an angle
from crossing $\pi/4$ for small $\eps$, so $P_s-P_0=O(\eps)$.
The negative selector $P_d$ of $h_d$ differs from $P$ by $O(\eps^2)$;
it commutes with $Q$ and $\|P_d-Q\|<1$, hence $P_d=Q$.
Conjugating resolvents by truncated $e^{\theta|x-x_0|}$ changes the
operator by $O(\theta)$; choosing $\theta$ below the actual contour
distance and taking the resolvent Neumann inverse gives exponential
kernel bounds. The resolvent identity now yields
\[
 P=P_0+P_1+R_2,\qquad |(R_2)_{xy}|\leq C\eps^2e^{-c|x-y|}.
\]
Crossing counts give
$\|T_A(R_2)\|_1\leq C\mathcal A\eps^2$ and
$\|T_A(P-P_0)\|_2^2\leq C\mathcal A\eps^2$.
For $Z(T)=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&T\\T^*&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$,
the entrywise Schatten inequality and exponential kernels give
$\Tr|Z(T_A(P))|^p+\Tr|Z(T_A(P_0))|^p\leq C\mathcal A p^{-4}$,
$0<p\leq1$. The power four is the three-dimensional radial sum
with one additional crossing distance.

Regularize $F(t)=g(|t|)$ only below $\eta$, so
$|F-F_\eta|\leq C\eta^{2-p}|t|^p$,
$\|F_\eta'\|\leq C$ and
$\|F_\eta''\|\leq C(1+|\log\eta|)$.
The finite-matrix trace Hessian is bounded by
$\|F_\eta''\|\|D\|_2^2$; its coefficients are divided differences
of $F_\eta'$. Onsite Dirac time reversal fixes the flat projector
and cut and reverses $P_1$, so its real linear trace vanishes.
The $R_2$ linear term is controlled in trace norm. Since
$S_A(P)=\frac12\Tr F(Z(T_A(P)))$, the total error is at most
\[
 C\mathcal A\{\eps^2(1+|\log\eta|)+\eta^{2-p}p^{-4}\}.
\]
Take $\eta=\eps$, $p=1/|\log\eps|$. This proves (1) without a gap
in the compressed covariance or a volume-rank entropy estimate.

\section{Massless-inclusive rate: explicit AP-parent and ground-sheet domain}
\textbf{Assumed domain.} Take even antiperiodic cubic parents of side
$L_p\asymp\eps^{-1}$, nonwrapping regular cuts with
$n_A\asymp\eps^{-3}$ and $\mathcal A\asymp\eps^{-2}$, and
$0\leq\mu\leq\mu_*$. The actual bounded real fields obey
\[
 \inf n-\sup|w|\geq c_*>0,\qquad
 \sum_q |q|_{\mathbb T}
   (|\widehat n(q)|+\sum_i|\widehat w_i(q)|)\leq M\eps.
\]
This is a native Fourier-transfer moment with aliases included; constant
fields cost zero. The actual occupied reference must equal the ground
selector $P_-(h)$, with any zero-level choice included in the low-energy
block. A continued sheet after occupation crossings is not automatically
that selector.

\textbf{Proved bound.} Uniformly in this mass interval and the stated
families,
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 \frac{|S_A(P)-S_A(P_0)|}{\mathcal A}\leq C\eps^{1/10}.} \tag{2}
\end{equation}
\textbf{Proof.} The exact lapse commutator transfer is
$(E_k-E_l)[1-h_kh_l/(E_kE_l)]/4$ and is $O(|k-l|_{\mathbb T})$.
For flow it is $(q_i(k)+q_i(l))(P_0(l)-P_0(k))/2$.
Away from nodes use $\|\nabla P_0\|\leq C/E$; near a node
$q_i=O(E)=O(|k-l|)$. Therefore $\|[h,P_0]\|\leq C\eps$ uniformly
in mass. With $h_b=P_0hP_0+(1-P_0)h(1-P_0)$,
$\|h-h_b\|\leq C\eps$ and
$\|h\psi\|,\|h_b\psi\|\geq c_*\|E\psi\|-C\eps\|\psi\|$.
The signed block forms are also controlled:
\[
 (1-P_0)h(1-P_0)\geq(1-P_0)(c_*E-C\eps)(1-P_0),\quad
 P_0hP_0\leq P_0(-c_*E+C\eps)P_0.
\]
To obtain these forms, replace the lapse by $E^{1/2}nE^{1/2}$
and flow by $E^{1/2}\{w_i,q_i/E\}E^{1/2}/2$.
The transfer errors are products of the uniformly one-half H\"older
functions $\sqrt E$ and $q_i/\sqrt E$, hence $O(|k-l|)$;
$\sum_i(q_i/E)^2\leq1$ bounds the flow form. This signed estimate,
not coercivity alone, controls the wrong occupied band.

Counting the eight AP-grid nodes and using min--max gives low-energy
rank $CL_p^3(\tau+\eps)^3$ for $h,h_b$, and wrong-sign rank
$CL_p^3\eps^3$ for $h_b$ relative to $P_0$. A smooth negative-energy
step and Fourier Duhamel then yield the distinct bounded/rank pieces
\[
 P-P_0=B_\tau+T_\tau,\quad
 \|B_\tau\|\leq C\eps/\tau,\quad
 \|T_\tau\|\leq3,\quad \rank T_\tau\leq CL_p^3(\tau+\eps)^3.
\]
Thus $\|T_A(P-P_0)\|_2\leq C\sqrt{n_A}d$,
$d=\eps/\tau+(\tau+\eps)^{3/2}$.
Dyadic nodal Fourier estimates, stopped at $L_p^{-1}$ before
periodization, give $|P_0(z)|\leq C(1+d_{\mathbb T}(z,0))^{-3}$.
Together with crossing counts this implies
$\|T_A(P_0)\|_{5/3}^{5/3}\leq C\mathcal A$.
Finite-rank interpolation bounds the difference's $5/3$ sum by
$Cn_Ad^{5/3}$. Min--max gives
$s_{2j-1}(X+Y)\leq s_j(X)+s_j(Y)$, so the perturbed sum has the
same two bounds. The singular-value square inequality
$\sum_j(s_j(X)-s_j(Y))^2\leq\|X-Y\|_2^2$, followed by
$|g'(t)|\leq Ct^{5/6}$ and Cauchy--Schwarz, gives
\[
 |S_A(P)-S_A(P_0)|\leq
 Cd\sqrt{n_A(\mathcal A+n_Ad^{5/3})}.
\]
Take $\tau=\eps^{2/5}$: $d=O(\eps^{3/5})$ and
$n_Ad^{5/3}=O(\mathcal A)$, proving (2).
The finite-matrix inequalities and the uniform dyadic/AP estimates are
proved explicitly in AP13--AP18; no unproved gapless entropy-Lipschitz
principle is used.

\section{Flat convex support and the axial-to-sphere ratio}
\textbf{Domain.} Fix $\mu\geq0$ and the same translation-invariant
flat pure $P_0$ on the infinite cubic parent. Use its established
one-face planar coefficient $\eta_\mu(n)$ and digital-ball theorem.
No varying background or subtraction between masses is used here.

\textbf{Boundary-cancelled proof.} For a finite cube $\Lambda_L$ set
\[
 b_L(B)=\tfrac12\{S(B)+S(\Lambda_L\setminus B)-S(\Lambda_L)\}.
\]
It is nonnegative, symmetric and submodular. In this Gaussian class
submodularity has a direct finite-matrix proof: integrate
\[
 h_2(u)=\int_0^\infty\log
       \frac{(u+t)(1-u+t)}{t(1+t)}\,dt.
\]
Each principal log determinant for $C+tI$ and $I-C+tI$ is submodular
by a Schur complement; the dimension term is modular. The integral
therefore proves the needed entropy inequality in the actual subsystem
convention. Diminishing marginal increments imply that the threshold
extension $\mathcal L_L(f)=\int b_L(\{f>t\})dt$ is the maximum
of its finitely many greedy linear forms, hence convex and even.

Approximate $h_2$ by symmetric endpoint-zero polynomials with
$|h_2-p|\leq\xi[u(1-u)]^\alpha$, $2/3<\alpha<1$.
The flat kernel and crossing counts give error $C\xi L^2$ uniformly
for every half-space slice of the cube and its complement. Integrated
over slicing levels and divided by cube volume, the error is at most
$C\xi|v|$. For a closed polynomial path with offsets $Y$, weight
$w(Y)$ and whole-cube anchor count $N_{\Lambda_L}(Y)$, the exact identity is
\[
 \int b_{L,p}(\Lambda_L\cap\{v\cdot x>t\})dt
 =-\frac12\sum_{j,Y}c_jw(Y)N_{\Lambda_L}(Y)
            [\max_Y v\cdot y-\min_Y v\cdot y].
\]
A path wholly inside the cube is split for exactly this level interval;
a path outside it occurs in none of the three traces. Thus outer faces
cancel exactly, before taking a limit. The first diameter moment is
absolutely summable: for $k(z)=(1+|z|)^{-3}$,
$k^{*j}(z)\leq C_jk(z)\log^{j-1}(2+|z|)$, and a marked closed-path
edge is bounded by $\sum_z|z|k(z)k^{*(j-1)}(-z)<\infty$.
Consequently $N_{\Lambda_L}/|\Lambda_L|\to1$ passes under the sum.
For rational normals the one-face coefficient is the negative sum of
the path maxima with the exact layer density; inversion of $P_0$
makes that sum half the range sum. Uniform polynomial approximation
extends the identification to every normal. Hence
\[
 \lim_{L\to\infty}\frac{\mathcal L_L(v\cdot x)}{|\Lambda_L|}
 =F_\mu(v)=|v|\eta_\mu(v/|v|),\qquad F_\mu(0)=0.
\]
This finite pointwise limit is convex, even and homogeneous, so it is
the support function of the compact centrally and cubic-symmetric body
$K_\mu=\{x:x\cdot v\leq F_\mu(v)\ \forall v\}$.
The supporting-functional construction and all uniform limits are given
in AP19--AP22. No sign of an individual path weight is assumed.

Let $a_\mu=\eta_\mu(e_1)>0$, a support value, not the lattice spacing.
Cubic symmetry and convexity place the axial cross-polytope inside
$K_\mu$ and the axial cube outside. Therefore
\[
 a_\mu\max_i|n_i|\leq\eta_\mu(n)\leq a_\mu\sum_i|n_i|.
\]
The two angular means are
$M_1=3\sqrt2\arctan(1/\sqrt2)/\pi$ and $3/2$.
The digital-ball theorem consequently gives
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 M_1a_\mu\leq\eta_{\rm sph}(\mu)\leq\tfrac32a_\mu,\qquad
 \frac{2L_0}{3a_\mu}\leq
 \frac{\eta_{\rm sph}(0)}{\eta_{\rm sph}(\mu)}
 \leq\frac{U_0}{M_1a_\mu}.}                         \tag{3}
\end{equation}
The ratio assumes valid inputs $0<L_0\leq\eta_{\rm sph}(0)\leq U_0$.
For $a_-\leq a_\mu\leq a_+$, use $a_+$ in its lower denominator
and $a_-$ in its upper denominator. No supplied numerical certificate
is proved here. Mass differences and finite-part derivatives need not
be convex support functions.

\section{Usable paper scope}
The two local-reference rates hold under their respective actual
global-gap or subthreshold AP-parent/ground-selector hypotheses.
They do not follow from the local symbol alone. For a limiting spherical
coefficient, add the separate flat-cut remainder
$|S_A(P_0)/\mathcal A-\eta_{\rm sph}(\mu)|$ and control the actual
parent limit. A physical evolved covariance still requires its separate
excitation estimate. Constants must be uniform over any time or allowed
averaging family being used. Equation (3) is a fixed-$ma$ flat theorem;
it neither evaluates an angular small-mass remainder nor solves the
fixed-physical-mass equation $a^2=4G\eta_{\rm sph}(ma)$.
These are proved analytic implications with assumed premises, not
validation of a production history or a new independent numerical route.

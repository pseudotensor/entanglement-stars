\paragraph{Result and usable qualification.}
The proposed \(0.5(\sigma/a)\sqrt{ma}\) has a precise
\emph{conditional} origin. Assume \(N=1\), zero radial momentum
source, a regular homogeneous averaged density, both positive
spin states filled in all eight momentum balls of radius
\(k_F=\pi/(4a)\), and the additional rest-energy closure
\(\rho=mn\). Phase-space counting and the radial Gauss row give
\[
 \frac{R_F^3}{a^3}=\frac{18N_B}{\pi^2},\qquad
 v=-Hr,\qquad H^2=\frac{8\pi G\rho}{3},\qquad
 \sigma|v'|=\frac{\pi}{6}\frac{\sigma}{a}\sqrt{ma}
       \quad(G/a^2=1/4).
\]
The number cancels because the specified filling fixes the
density; \(R_F\) is a volume-comparison radius, not the recorded
energy centroid or a trapping root. At the ideal sharp \(N=1\)
vacuum match, \(v'\) changes from \(-H\) inside to \(+H/2\)
outside: opposite sign and half the radial magnitude.
The tangential eigenvalues remain \(-H\), so the full spherical
strain operator norm remains \(H\); this does not halve that
gate or describe an averaged transition layer.
For a general core the lapse, local mass slope, current and
signed reference source remain; number alone fixes no gradient.

The small-mass extrapolation is not controlled at this momentum.
For the same native sine-band filling, its mean normal energy
\(\bar e/a\) satisfies
\[
 \bar e\ge\sqrt{(ma)^2+9/32},\qquad
 \sigma|v'|=\frac{\pi}{6}\frac{\sigma}{a}\sqrt{\bar e}
       >0.38\,\frac{\sigma}{a}.
\]
Thus the homogeneous filled-source comparison fails the
\(0.1\) one-term gate for \(\sigma/a\ge2\), even as \(ma\to0\).
The actual energy-defined pocket has a separate analytic
lower bound with the same conclusion (C8). These are conditional
positive-source comparisons, not a prohibition on a differently
occupied, nonuniform or reference-subtracted core.
Moreover, the uniform-field mean velocity of the spherical
filling at \(ak_F=\pi/4\) is \(24v_i/\pi^3\), still inward:
the axial mode's vanishing advective derivative is not a bulk
counter-advection or rebound criterion.

Independently, the supplied \(d=16\) continuum first-maximum
coefficient \(c_E=0.135858\) implies, for the actual charge
\(r_s=2GE\),
\[
 E>E_{\max}^{\rm cont}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \frac{r_s}{a}>\frac{2c_E}{\sqrt g\,(ma)^2},
 \qquad g=\frac G{a^2}.
\]
At \(g=1/4,\sigma/a=2\), combining this only with the
rest-energy estimate gives \(ma\le9/(100\pi^2)\) and
\(r_s/a>40000c_E\pi^4/81\), of order \(6.5\,10^3\).
This explains the order-\(10^4\) estimate as a dust-comparator
wedge; it does not establish a native hydrodynamic regime,
because that closure omits order-\(a^{-1}\) kinetic energy.
The continuum maximum is not a lattice turning-point or
collapse theorem. The stronger geometry, averaging, source
and evolution requirements remain, as do the recorded
sampled-gate failures and uncomputed later outcome.

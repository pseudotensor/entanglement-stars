### 4.2 Not excluded by the tables: the lift's propagated error

Section 9 and the comparator add the lift's Gram statistic to the error sum as a weight error of 2.3e-2 or 3.5e-2 at every clock. That is the plan's rule; it is exact in no sense beyond t 0 (where the shell weights of the lifted column differ from the chain's by amounts the statistic covers; section 2 item 7(b) shows 0.015 and 0.006 beyond r_h), and it is not a bound on how an initial identification error propagates. A difference of amplitude epsilon in the initial state becomes, under a unitary propagation of the same operator, a weight difference of at most 2 sqrt(p) epsilon + epsilon^2 in any shell. What epsilon is, the review states and version 1 got wrong at its lower end: the amplitude error of the lifted state against its closest isometric image is first order in the lift's Gram deviation (the polar part's square root differs from the identity by half the deviation to first order, derived), not second order as version 1 wrote; and the printed statistic is column-weighted over the shells, not a uniform bound on that deviation, whose support maximum is 0.1977 for the source and 0.5097 for the detector (the review's reading of the lift's files; the md prints only the latter, as the detector band's term). So epsilon runs from the order of the column-weighted statistic, 0.023 and 0.035 (the lower end, a coefficient of order 1 where the polar reading gives 1/2), to the order of the support maximum, 0.1977 (the upper end); version 1's pessimistic square-root reading, 0.152 and 0.188, lands at that upper end's size and its "second-order, far smaller" lower end does not exist. The weight bound 2 sqrt(p) epsilon + epsilon^2 (derived): at the lower end 0.021, 0.033, 0.042 at p 0.2, 0.5, 0.8 for R_c 20 and 0.033, 0.051, 0.064 for R_c 14, that is 0.9 to 1.8 times the error sum; at the upper end 0.22, 0.32, 0.39 at p 0.2, 0.5, 0.8, of the order of the largest residuals 0.33 and 0.34 (at p 0.4 to 0.8) and above every residual at the shells beyond 1.5 r_s (the largest there 0.144).
